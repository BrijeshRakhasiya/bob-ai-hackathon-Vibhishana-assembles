# Problem Statement

## Who is affected

Dispatchers and operations managers at domestic freight/logistics companies in India — the people responsible for tracking active shipments, reacting to disruptions (highway closures, monsoon flooding, port strikes, fuel shortages), and deciding whether to reroute a shipment or redeploy fleet assets.

## The problem, in depth

Supply chain disruptions cascade across hundreds of active shipments in ways that are effectively impossible to track manually:

- **Disruption awareness is reactive, not proactive.** A dispatcher typically learns about a highway closure or flooding event from a phone call, a news alert, or a WhatsApp group — not from a system that has already cross-referenced it against every shipment currently in transit through that corridor.
- **Fleet sits idle in the wrong place.** While one region faces surge demand (e.g. a rerouted lane suddenly needs extra capacity), idle trucks in another depot go unused, because there's no systematic way to match idle assets to at-risk lanes in real time.
- **Cold-chain and time-critical cargo carry outsized risk.** A single temperature excursion on a vaccine shipment, or a missed JIT delivery window for an assembly line, can spoil or devalue cargo worth lakhs — but today these breaches are typically only discovered at delivery, when it's too late to act.
- **Reroute decisions lack real comparison.** Even when a dispatcher knows a shipment needs to be rerouted, there's rarely a clean, fast way to compare 2–3 alternative carriers or corridors on price, ETA, and reliability before committing.

## Why existing solutions don't solve it

Most logistics operations still rely on spreadsheets, phone calls, and a TMS (transport management system) that tracks *where a shipment is*, but not *what's about to go wrong with it*. Off-the-shelf TMS platforms are built around tracking and invoicing, not disruption correlation or negotiation.

## Quantified pain (industry-level reference points)

- The 2021 LA/Long Beach port backlog cost global supply chains an estimated $10B+ — a comparable-scale event on Indian rail/road corridors during monsoon season causes similar cascading delay, at a domestic scale.
- A single temperature excursion on a cold-chain shipment (vaccines, perishables) can spoil cargo worth ₹50L+ in one incident.
- Idle fleet utilisation below 60–70% is common industry-wide when redeployment is done manually rather than systematically.

## Why this problem matters now

India's domestic freight volumes are growing quickly, monsoon-driven disruptions are a predictable annual pattern (not a black swan), and AI copilots (like IBM Bob) make it newly practical for a small team to build a disruption-correlation and negotiation system that would previously have required a much larger engineering effort.
